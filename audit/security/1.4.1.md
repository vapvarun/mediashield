# MediaShield 1.4.1 security review

Scope: Free and Pro, `v1.4.0..1.4.1`. Mechanical sweep (G1), a permission matrix run as six roles on a Free + Pro Docker site (G2), an independent reviewed diff (G3) and an independent browser walk on two Docker sites (Free alone, Free + Pro). Every finding below was reproduced before it was fixed, and each fix has a unit test that fails on the old code.

## Permission matrix (G2)
Every `/mediashield` and `/mediashield-pro` GET route was dispatched as a visitor, two subscribers, an author, an editor and an admin, with ids filled from seeded objects.

- Every admin, analytics, export, platform and Pro route answers 401 to a visitor and 403 to a subscriber, author and editor.
- `analytics/users/{id}` (another viewer's history) is admin-only.
- Public by design: `POST /mediashield-pro/v1/bunny/webhook` (Bunny cannot log in; the handler checks the signature or token before anything else) and `GET /playlists/{id}/player/{video}` (published items only, 404 while a playlist password is required).
- The two inline permission callbacks (`digest/send-test`, `wizard/complete`) require `manage_options`.
- `PATCH /mediashield-pro/v1/platforms/{id}` (Bunny Read-Only key): 401 visitor, 403 subscriber and editor, 200 admin; the key is stored encrypted, other `extra_config` keys survive, non-Bunny and missing ids are refused, and neither response echoes the key.
- A logged-out visitor's signed `/stream/{id}` address answers 403 "Please log in" under Require Login.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | High | `GET /wp/v2/mediashield-videos` returned every video's `_ms_source_url` (the direct uploads address for a self-hosted file), `_ms_platform_video_id`, `_ms_stream_url` and `_ms_access_role` to anyone. | 1.4.0 and earlier | `rest_prepare_mediashield_video` drops `meta` unless the user can `edit_posts`. The Video block (authors and up) keeps it. |
| 2 | High | The player printed the provider id and source URL of a video the viewer may not watch (a Bunny embed or an unlisted Vimeo id plays on its own). 1.4.1's playlist pages put this on every published playlist; the auto-wrapped core embed path did the same. | 1.4.0 and earlier | `Renderer::withhold_addresses()`, called by `Renderer::render()` and `PlayerWrapper`, blanks the provider id, source URL and stream URL when `can_watch()` denies and marks the player `data-access-denied`. The script shows the login prompt to a visitor and asks `/session/start` for the reason otherwise. |
| 3 | Medium | `GET /playlists/{id}/items` asked only `is_user_logged_in()`, so any subscriber read every playlist's items with each video's source URL, including password-protected playlists. | 1.4.0 and earlier | Requires `edit_posts`, like the item writes. Every reader is an editing screen. |
| 4 | Medium | A drip- or sequence-locked Learnomy lesson still carried a working MediaShield embed link in its REST payload. | 1.3.0 | `Integrations\Learnomy::add_embed_to_payload()` skips a payload marked `locked`. |

| 6 | Low (data loss) | `wp mediashield scale teardown` and the Pro equivalent deleted every row with `user_id >= 1000000`, real members included on a large site. CLI only. | 1.3.0 | Deletes only rows whose user does not exist; the seeder creates no users. |

## Accepted by the owner
- **Instructor free previews open a video to guests (2026-11-04).** Learnomy's `mediashield_guest_can_watch` hook admits guests to a video when any published free-preview lesson plays it, so an instructor can open a library video they do not own past Require Login. The owner accepts this: who becomes an instructor, and what they may publish, is the site admin's choice. MediaShield's role and LMS gates still deny guests where set.

## Scanner-visible but safe
- `sql-direct-interpolated`: table names from `$wpdb->prefix` plus fixed names; values go through `prepare()`. The My Videos `HAVING` text is picked from an allow-list in the code.
- Bunny webhook `permission_callback => __return_true`: Bunny cannot authenticate; the handler compares an HMAC over the raw body with `hash_equals`, or the URL token, before parsing or writing.
- The webhook URL shown in Pro settings carries the `ms_token`: it is how Bunny proves itself when no signing key is saved, and it is only returned by an admin-only, no-store route.
- `data-guest-allowed` and `data-access-denied` are hints to the script; `/session/start` and `/stream` decide access on the server.
- `litespeed_control_set_nocache` without our prefix is LiteSpeed's own hook.
- WP-CLI commands register only under `WP_CLI` and carry the ABSPATH check.
- Supersedes the 1.4.0 note that provider ids visible to visitors are safe: for a viewer who may not watch, they are now withheld.
